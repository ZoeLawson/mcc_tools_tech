## Core Principles
<!-- Unordered list with paragraph-aligned items -->

- **Use active voice**  
  Writing in active voice improves clarity and reduces ambiguity. Prefer direct phrasing that clearly identifies the actor.

- **Address the reader directly**  
  Use second person (“you”) carefully to keep instructions actionable without sounding repetitive or accusatory.

- **Distinguish actions from system responses**  
  Separate what the user *does* from what the system *returns* to avoid confusion in procedural steps.

- **Write in present tense**  
  Technical processes typically occur immediately, so present indicative keeps instructions precise and consistent.



## Examples
<!-- Ordered list with paragraphs -->

1. **Active vs Passive Voice**  
   Active voice improves readability:
   - **YES:** *You press the function key to edit.*  
   - **NO:** *The function key is pressed to perform editing.*

2. **Second Person Usage**
   Avoid redundancy:
   - **YES:** Use concise task grouping  
   - **NO:** Repeating “you can…” in every bullet

3. **Clarity in Instructions**  
   Separate instructions from outcomes for better comprehension.
